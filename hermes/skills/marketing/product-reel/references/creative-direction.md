# Creative direction

Adapt these patterns to the brief. They are not a mandatory style.

## Shots that have a purpose

| Shot | Purpose | Useful motion |
|---|---|---|
| Studio hero | Product recognition and hook | Small push-in, rigid bob, surrounding props drifting |
| Sensory detail | Flavor, texture, material, use | Bubbles, light glints, restrained camera slide |
| Use occasion | Product in the audience's world | Slow push-in, parallax, environmental movement |
| Detail crop | Rhythm between longer shots | Deterministic punch-in from a sharp source |
| Closing hero | Recall and action | Stable or slowed shot with copy clearance |

Choose props supported by the product and concept. Do not imply an unsupported ingredient, benefit, or endorsement. Hands, pouring, spinning labels, and complex interactions can be worthwhile but demand closer review.

## Still prompt pattern

```text
Use case: ads-marketing.
Asset: one vertical campaign photograph for [product] and [audience].
Input image: the exact product reference.
Preserve: [brand, variant, wordmark, artwork, colors, proportions, materials].
Scene: [one concrete studio or lifestyle scene].
Composition: [product position/scale]; [specific empty region for later copy].
Lighting/materials: [concrete photographic treatment].
Palette: [derived from the product or brief].
No added headlines, captions, watermark, collage, or invented label.
Keep key product details inside the intended platform-safe region.
```

Reference fidelity is an instruction, not a guarantee. Review small labels and variant names.

## Motion prompt pattern

```text
Animate the supplied campaign image as a product shot.
Motion: [one modest camera action] plus [one subtle environmental action].
Product stays rigid; label faces the viewer and retains exact typography.
Preserve existing objects, lighting, and scene composition.
Maintain the empty headline area throughout the shot.
No added text, new objects, unrequested people, cuts, or label rotation.
```

Use constraints relevant to the shot. A retry should address the observed defect.

## Audio

Specify duration, approximate BPM, instrumentation, mood, vocal policy, and ending. For an instrumental, explicitly exclude lyrics, speech, and vocal ad-libs. An immediate groove, midpoint lift, and final accent can support a short ad.

Check the output: the reference project requested 20 seconds but received about 24.6 seconds. Trim and align the actual track.

## Working example: OLIPOP Strawberry Vanilla

A reference, not a universal template:
- Audience: young adult women.
- Idea: a small afternoon treat becomes an appealing everyday ritual.
- Product-derived palette: coral, blush, cream, forest green.
- Scenes: floating can/strawberries; sunlit glass/can; gingham picnic.
- Copy: “little treat. big mood.” → “strawberry & vanilla.” → “take your break outside.” → “make it a soda date.”
- Edit: 20 seconds, 1080×1920; three five-second motion clips reused for detail cuts and closing.
- Audio: original instrumental pop-house near 120 BPM, with a quiet can-opening effect.
- Type: rounded sans serif paired with italic serif.
- Important correction: the rising can collided with the headline near the end. Smaller/higher copy and a slowed earlier section restored clearance.
- Important correction: removing pale small captions over close-ups improved contrast and visual breathing room.

No additional health claims were introduced. The export was checked for 600 frames, 20-second duration, H.264/AAC playback, audio levels, and beginning/end readability.
